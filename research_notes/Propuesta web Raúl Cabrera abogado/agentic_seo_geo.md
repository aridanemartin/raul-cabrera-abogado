## 1. Google AI Overviews and AI Mode in Spain; other AI engines (market share in Spain, how they pick and cite sources)

### Takeaway
Both Google AI surfaces are live in Spain and in Spanish: AI Overviews ("Vista creada con IA") since about spring 2025, and AI Mode ("Modo IA") since 8 October 2025. Google's agentic features (booking, AI calling businesses, Gemini auto-browse) are still US-first and not available in Spain/EEA. AI Overviews are linked to large organic CTR drops (−34% to −61% depending on the study). Still, AI assistants send Spanish sites only about 0.3% of total traffic, and ChatGPT dominates that share (about 70%), with Gemini and Perplexity growing. Each engine draws on a different index: Google Search (AIO, AI Mode, Gemini), Bing (Copilot), Brave (Claude), its own crawler (Perplexity, ChatGPT's OAI-SearchBot). So the site has to be crawlable and indexable across all of them, not only in Google.

### Cited Findings

**Google AI Overviews in Spain**
- By 2 April 2025 Maldita.es was describing AI Overviews ("resumen creado con IA") as available in Spain. It called them unreliable as a source, liable to contain errors, and shown "only when systems determine they will be most useful" **[2025]**: [Maldita.es](https://maldita.es/malditatecnologia/20250402/resumen-creado-ia-google-desinformacion/); [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/robotica-e-ia/resultados-google-ia-generativa-llegan-a-espana-ellos-elefante-habitacion-para-medios)
- At launch in Spain, AI Overviews appeared for Spanish and English queries, only for signed-in users aged 18+. This launch-time restriction may since have changed and was not re-verified **[2025]**: [TreceBits](https://www.trecebits.com/resultados-busqueda-ia-google-espana/)
- I found no primary Google announcement giving the exact Spain launch date for AI Overviews (the commonly repeated "March 2025" is unverified; see Gaps).

**Google AI Mode in Spain**
- On 23 Sept 2025 AI Mode launched in Spanish worldwide, but only "where AI Mode is live" **[2025]**: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/23/googles-ai-mode-arrives-in-spanish-globally/). Spain was initially excluded pending EU clearance: [Montevideo Portal](https://www.montevideo.com.uy/Ciencia-y-Tecnologia/Llego-el-buscador-de-Google-en-espanol-ya-incorpora-modo-IA--uc937721)
- Google Spain's official blog, dated 8 Oct 2025, says the rollout of AI Mode covers 36 new languages and about 50 new countries, including Spain, activated progressively. Google's own caveat: "no siempre acertaremos" **[2025]**: [Google Blog España](https://blog.google/intl/es-es/productos/la-busqueda-de-google-el-modo-ia-llega-a-espana/); [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/robotica-e-ia/google-lanza-espana-su-modo-ia-ello-nueva-forma-realizar-busquedas-web-asi-puedes-activarlo); [Maldita.es](https://maldita.es/malditatecnologia/20251010/modo-ia-google-contrastar-respuestas/)
- AI Mode's agentic expansion to 180 countries in August 2025 was English-only and excluded the EU **[2025]**: [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-is-making-ai-mode-in-search-more-agentic-and-launching-it-in-over-180-new-countries/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/google-ai-mode-is-expanding-to-180-countries-and-adding-an-agentic-restaurant-finder-154833417.html). Spanish trade press noted that 2026 AI Mode novelties expanded "pero no a España" **[2026]**: [Marketing4eCommerce](https://marketing4ecommerce.net/ai-mode-novedades-nuevos-paises/)

**Measured impact on organic CTR (2025–2026 studies)**
- **Seer Interactive** (Sept 2025 update; about 3,119 informational queries, 42 organisations): organic CTR on queries with an AI Overview fell from 1.76% (June 2024) to 0.61% (Sept 2025), about −61%. Organic CTR on queries without an AIO also fell, from 2.74% to 1.62%. Brands cited inside the AIO had 35% higher organic CTR (0.70% vs 0.52%, Q3 2025), but Seer says it "cannot definitively prove that citation causes higher CTRs" **[2025]**: [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-drive-drop-organic-paid-ctr-464212)
- **Ahrefs** (4 Feb 2026 update, December 2025 data, 300k keywords): an AI Overview correlates with 58% lower average CTR for the #1 result, up from the 34.5% drop measured in its April 2025 version. Drops are smaller further down (about 50.8% at position 2, about 19.4% at position 10). The comparison is against a modelled baseline, so it is correlational **[2026]**: [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)
- **Pew Research** (US panel, 68,879 searches, data March 2025): users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits with an AI summary vs 15% without. Only 1% clicked a link inside the summary. Sessions ended more often after an AI summary (26% vs 16%) **[2025]**: [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/pew-google-ai-overviews-39808.html); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/pew-finds-that-only-1-percent-of-users-click-a-source-link-directly-from-googles-ai-overviews/)
- Google's own position is that AI Overview clicks are "higher quality" (users spend more time on site) **[2026]**: [Google Search Central: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- A claimed Seer "recovery" (AIO CTR 1.3% → 2.4%, Dec 2025 to Feb 2026) appears only in a secondary article attributing it to "Seer International". Unverified **[SECONDARY]**: [Digitaleer](https://www.digitaleer.com/ai-impact-google-search-traffic-2026/)

**Prevalence on legal queries**
- Ahrefs (146M SERPs, Sept 2025 desktop): 34.3% of YMYL queries trigger an AIO vs a 20.5% baseline. Legal is about 23.6%, medical 44.1% **[2025]**: [Ahrefs: What triggers AI Overviews](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/)
- SE Ranking (about 1,200 YMYL keywords): legal had the highest AIO rate of the YMYL categories at 77.67%. This is a much smaller sample and conflicts with Ahrefs **[2025]**: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-ai-overviews-avoid-political-content-new-data-shows/529244/)
- No Spain- or Spanish-language-specific measurement of AIO prevalence on legal queries was found (see Gaps).

**AI assistants' traffic and market share in Spain**
- SE Ranking (101,574 sites, Jan 2025–Apr 2026, published 17 June 2026): AI referral traffic was 0.23% of all traffic in Spain in 2025 and 0.30% in Jan–Apr 2026 (global 0.32%). Share of AI referrals in Spain in 2026: ChatGPT 70.49% (down from 81.44%), Gemini 12.41% (from 4.39%), Perplexity 11.64%, Copilot 3.32%, Claude 1.82% (+511% YoY), Mistral 0.15%, Meta AI 0.13%. Organic search fell from 52.02% to 40.70% of traffic in Spain **[2026]**: [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/es/blog/trafico-ia-espana/)
- StatCounter AI-chatbot share for Spain (month labels inconsistent across pages): in the June 2026 view, ChatGPT 74.6%, Gemini 10.41%, Perplexity 5.52%, Copilot 5.13%, Claude 4.3% **[2026][SECONDARY reading of the StatCounter page]**: [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/ai-chatbot-market-share/all/spain)
- Whitespark notes ChatGPT handles only about 2.8% of (local) searches and advises against over-focusing on it **[SECONDARY]**: [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/blog/chatgpt-sucks-at-local-seo)

**How each engine selects sources and which index it uses**
- **Google AI Overviews / AI Mode:** to be a supporting link, a page must be "indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet". There are "no additional requirements … nor other special optimizations necessary". AI feature traffic is reported inside Search Console's "Web" search type **[2026]**: [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). Google describes its generative features as retrieval-augmented generation on top of Search ranking systems, and warns against making pages for every "fan-out" query: [Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- **Gemini app:** grounding uses "content from the Google Search index", controllable through the Google-Extended token: [Google crawlers doc](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/google-common-crawlers)
- **ChatGPT search:** inclusion depends on OAI-SearchBot. "Sites that are opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers" (they may still appear as navigational links). Changes take about 24h: [OpenAI bots docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots). OpenAI does not publicly document which third-party index (if any) supplements its own (see Gaps). A third-party local-SEO analysis claims ChatGPT local recommendations take Bing rankings into account **[SECONDARY]**: [Loganix](https://loganix.com/google-local-search-ranking-factors/)
- **Claude:** web search appears to run on Brave Search. Identical citations, a "BraveSearchParams" parameter, and Brave was added to Anthropic's subprocessor list on 19 Mar 2025 **[2025]**: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/21/anthropic-appears-to-be-using-brave-to-power-web-searches-for-its-claude-chatbot/); [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Mar/20/claude-can-now-search-the-web/). Anthropic also runs Claude-SearchBot (indexing for search quality) and Claude-User (user-initiated fetches): [Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler)
- **Perplexity:** its own index via PerplexityBot ("designed to surface and link websites in search results … not used to crawl content for AI foundation models"). Perplexity-User fetches pages live on user request and "generally ignores robots.txt rules": [Perplexity docs](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots)
- **Microsoft Copilot:** Bing. Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report (public preview Feb 2026) tracks citations in Copilot, Bing AI summaries and "select AI partner integrations" **[2026]**: [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/bing-webmaster-tools-testing-new-ai-performance-report-468039); [Search Influence](https://www.searchinfluence.com/blog/bing-ai-performance-report-copilot-citations/)

### Inferences
- For a Spanish law firm, Google (classic results + AIO + AI Mode + Maps/GBP) is still the main channel by a wide margin. AI assistants are a small but fast-growing referral source (about 0.3% of traffic in Spain), so the service should be sold as "future-proofing + brand accuracy in AI answers", not as a primary lead source today.
- Because indexes differ (Google, Bing, Brave, Perplexity's own, OpenAI's own), "agentic SEO" in practice means: be indexable in Google and Bing, be crawlable by every AI search/retrieval bot, and be present in the third-party sources these engines retrieve (directories, press, GBP, reviews).
- Spain/EU users will see agentic Google features (booking, AI calling, auto-browse) later than US users, if at all. Anything sold on those features is speculative for 2026–2027.

### Gaps
- No primary Google source for the exact AI Overviews launch date in Spain (secondary sources point to roughly late March / early April 2025).
- No Spain-specific or Spanish-language data on AIO/AI Mode prevalence or CTR impact for legal queries ("abogado divorcio Madrid", "despido improcedente", etc.).
- OpenAI's ChatGPT search help and launch pages returned HTTP 403, so the exact third-party search providers behind ChatGPT search could not be verified. Third-party claims conflict (Bing vs own index + Google results).
- No reliable AI Mode usage share for Spain was found.

## 2. On-site and off-site signals with evidence of increasing AI citations

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence points to **off-site brand mentions/authority** (Ahrefs: branded web mentions ρ≈0.66 with AIO visibility vs backlinks ρ≈0.22), **being indexed and ranking well in the underlying search engines**, and content that **adds statistics, quotations and citations** (Princeton GEO lab study, up to +40% visibility). **Structured data shows no causal uplift** in the best controlled test (Ahrefs, May 2026). **Freshness** helps modestly (cited URLs about 25.7% fresher). For local businesses, on-page signals, reviews and citations dominate AI local visibility (Whitespark 2026, secondary). Google's official May 2026 guide says AEO/GEO "is still SEO" and explicitly discounts chunking, llms.txt, special schema and inauthentic mentions.

### Cited Findings

**Google's official stance (most important primary source)**
- "Optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." Also: "There's no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces for AI"; "Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add"; and "seeking inauthentic 'mentions' across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem". Don't create pages for every fan-out query "primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses" (scaled-content abuse). Merchant Center feeds and Google Business Profiles "can help products and services appear in AI responses" **[2026; reportedly published 15 May 2026]**: [Google: Optimizing your website for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide); [Search Engine Journal coverage](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-new-ai-search-guide-calls-aeo-and-geo-still-seo/575026/)
- Google's AI features page: keep "Business Profile information current"; keep important content in text; structured data must match visible text **[2026]**: [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

**Brand mentions and co-citations**
- Ahrefs (about 75,000 brands, AI Overviews): Spearman correlations were branded web mentions 0.664, branded anchors 0.527, branded search volume 0.392, Domain Rating 0.326, backlinks 0.218, branded ad traffic 0.216. Top-quartile brands by mentions averaged 169 AIO mentions vs 14 in the next quartile. "Correlation ≠ causation" **[2025]**: [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)
- A Seer Interactive study is reported to find weak link correlations for ChatGPT brand mentions (domain rank 0.25, backlinks 0.10) **[SECONDARY]**: [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@lorenzoswanson786/ahrefs-studied-75-000-brands-brand-mentions-beat-backlinks-3-to-1-for-ai-visibility-e86ef9f6baba)
- In a US legal-sector study, directories and rankings made up 21.3% of citations for "law firm" questions, and official/government sources 3.5% **[2026][PR-agency research]**: [5W AmLaw baseline](https://5wpr.com/research/legal/baseline/)

**Passage-level citability (answer-first, statistics, quotes)**
- Princeton/IIT Delhi "GEO" paper (KDD 2024; arXiv 2311.09735): GEO methods boost visibility "by up to 40%" on their benchmark and up to 37% on live Perplexity. Adding citations, quotations from relevant sources and statistics were the best-performing methods; keyword stuffing did not help **[OLDER: 2023–2024, lab benchmark, pre-AI-Mode]**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735). The per-method figures circulating in blogs (e.g., "+41% quotations") vary by metric and source and should be checked against the paper **[SECONDARY]**: [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/generative-engine-optimization-paper)
- Google says Search systems "are able to understand the nuance of multiple topics on a page", so no special chunking is required **[2026]**: [Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

**Structured data / schema**
- Ahrefs controlled study (published 11 May 2026; 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD between Aug 2025 and Mar 2026 vs about 4,000 matched controls; difference-in-differences): "Adding schema produced no major uplift in citations on any platform." ChatGPT +2.2% and AI Mode +2.4% (not significant); AI Overviews −4.6% (small, significant, cause unknown). Caveat: all pages were already heavily cited, the window was 30 days, and schema types were pooled. Ahrefs' earlier observational finding (cited pages about 3× likelier to have JSON-LD) is attributed to confounding **[2026]**: [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)
- A searchVIU experiment (Oct 2025) found major AI systems ignored schema when retrieving live pages (e.g., a price present only in JSON-LD was not extracted) **[2025][via Ahrefs]**: [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)

**Freshness**
- Ahrefs (about 17M citations across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot, Gemini, AIO): AI-cited URLs average 1,064 days old vs 1,432 for organic SERPs (about 25.7% fresher). By last update, 909 vs 1,047 days. ChatGPT shows the strongest freshness preference; AI Overviews cite slightly older pages than organic. Long-lived content still dominates (average 2.9 years) **[2025]**: [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/)
- A Sept 2026 CiteSurge study reportedly found a date within the last three months did not materially change citation rates in Claude or Grok **[2026][SECONDARY, unverified]**: [Omnius summary](https://www.omnius.so/blog/does-content-freshness-influence-ai-search)

**Local / entity signals (most relevant to a law firm)**
- Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors (47 experts, 187 factors) added a separate "AI Search Visibility" category: on-page signals 24%, review signals 16%, citation signals 13%, GBP about 12%. Review recency and velocity matter more than volume **[2026][SECONDARY, expert survey not experiment]**: [Reputation.com](https://reputation.com/resources/articles/whitespark-2026-three-insights-every-brand-should-know); [Loganix](https://loganix.com/google-local-search-ranking-factors/)
- ChatGPT recommends only about 1.2% of local business locations (SOCi). 83% of restaurants were invisible in ChatGPT vs 14% in Google (Local Falcon, Mar 2026). ChatGPT reportedly draws 58% of its local info from business websites **[2026][SECONDARY]**: [Loganix](https://loganix.com/google-local-search-ranking-factors/)
- Spanish legal-marketing guidance (LegalToday, Dec 2025): AI answers are built from websites, profiles, reviews and directories. NAP (name, address, phone, specialties) must be identical everywhere. Reviews, press mentions, legal rankings, GBP, LinkedIn (firm and partners) and articles in specialised media act as authority signals **[2025][opinion/practitioner]**: [LegalToday](https://www.legaltoday.com/gestion-del-despacho/marketing-para-abogados/como-adaptarse-a-la-nueva-busqueda-con-ia-claves-para-que-tu-despacho-sea-visible-en-todos-los-canales-2025-12-09/)

**Being cited correlates with better outcomes**
- Seer: brands cited in AIO get 35% higher organic CTR than non-cited brands (correlational) **[2025]**: [Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update)

**Conversion value of AI referrals (conflicting)**
- Semrush (July 2025): the average AI-search visitor is "4.4×" as valuable as an organic visitor (modelled, digital-marketing topics) **[2025]**: [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-search-seo-traffic-study/). An analysis of 973 ecommerce sites found ChatGPT referrals convert worse than Google search **[2025]**: [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/llms-google-referral-conversion-study-463747). A separate 94-site study found ChatGPT 1.81% vs non-branded organic 1.39% **[2026]**: [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-vs-non-branded-organic-search-conversions-470321)

### Inferences
- Evidence ranking for a small law firm: (1) classic SEO/indexability plus a strong GBP and reviews (PROVEN); (2) off-site mentions in local press, legal media, directories and the Colegio de Abogados census (strong correlational evidence; real mentions only); (3) answer-first, sourced, data-rich content written from first-hand practice (lab evidence + Google's "people-first" guidance); (4) freshness and visible update dates (modest); (5) schema as hygiene for rich results and entity disambiguation, not as an AI-citation lever.
- "FAQ blocks" and "author E-E-A-T" are best framed as good content practice. I found no study isolating their effect on AI citations.
- Selling point for the client: "we make your firm the best-documented, most consistent entity in your city/practice area across the sources AI engines read". That is defensible. "We get you into ChatGPT" is not guaranteed.

### Gaps
- No controlled study isolating author/E-E-A-T markup, FAQ sections or `sameAs`/Wikidata on AI citations was found. Wikidata's effect on AI visibility for small businesses is unstudied in the sources reviewed (and a small local firm is unlikely to meet Wikidata notability anyway, which is an inference).
- Unverified recollection, not re-checked in this research: Google restricted FAQ rich results to authoritative government/health sites in Aug 2023. Verify before advising on FAQPage markup.
- The Princeton per-tactic percentages should be read from the paper's tables. Only the abstract-level figures were verified.
- No Spanish-language replication of any of these studies.

## 3. llms.txt, robots.txt for AI crawlers, and Cloudflare's AI-bot defaults

### Takeaway
**llms.txt is HYPE for search visibility.** Google says Search doesn't use it, no major AI engine has confirmed using it for search or citations, and Ahrefs' log study found 97% of llms.txt files got zero requests (AI search/retrieval bots were about 1% of the requests that did happen). It costs little and can't hurt, so it is fine as an extra, not as a deliverable. **robots.txt strategy matters more:** each vendor now separates *training* bots from *search/retrieval* bots, so a firm can block training while staying visible in AI answers. The biggest practical risk for a small site is **CDN defaults**: Cloudflare has blocked AI crawlers by default for new sign-ups since July 2025, and from 15 Sept 2026 its defaults also block "mixed-use" crawlers on ad-bearing pages for free/new sites. A law firm on Cloudflare must explicitly allow AI search bots.

### Cited Findings

**llms.txt**
- Google: Search "doesn't use" llms.txt. It won't help or hurt visibility, and you don't need "new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown" **[2026]**: [Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). John Mueller called it "not done for search" and a "temporary crutch, perhaps to save some tokens" for AI coding tools **[2026]**: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/97-of-llms-txt-files-got-no-requests-ahrefs-data-shows/579478/)
- Ahrefs log study (about 137k domains, May 2026): about 28% of (tech-skewed) domains publish llms.txt. 97% of files received zero requests; about 1,100 of about 38,000 received any. Requests came mostly from SEO audit tools (21%), unidentified bots (14%), web crawlers (13%) and tech profilers (11%). AI bots made up 19% (coding agents 10%, training crawlers 5%, assistants 2%), and ChatGPT/Perplexity retrieval bots about 1%. Claude-Code and GPTBot were the top AI fetchers, and Slackbot fetched more than PerplexityBot. Ahrefs flags auto-generated llms.txt as a possible prompt-injection surface **[2026]**: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/97-of-llms-txt-files-got-no-requests-ahrefs-data-shows/579478/)
- Adoption is growing (Originality.ai: 4,088 → 36,120 instances, June 2025 → May 2026, 3M+ sites tracked), but one about 300k-domain study found "no clear effect on AI citations" **[2026][SECONDARY]**: [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/llms-txt-adoption-rises-8-8x-but-97-of-files-get-zero-ai-requests/)
- Anthropic's crawler documentation does not mention llms.txt: [Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler). Anthropic and OpenAI publish llms.txt for their own developer docs but have not said their crawlers use other sites' files **[SECONDARY]**: [Hoverify](https://tryhoverify.com/blog/llms-txt-what-it-is-and-who-reads-it/)

**Vendor bot taxonomy (training vs search vs user-initiated)**
- **OpenAI:** *GPTBot* = training ("Disallowing GPTBot indicates a site's content should not be used in training"). *OAI-SearchBot* = ChatGPT search inclusion, independent of the GPTBot setting. *ChatGPT-User* = user-initiated actions ("robots.txt rules may not apply"; "not used to determine whether content may appear in Search"). *OAI-AdsBot* = ad landing-page safety checks, not training **[2026]**: [OpenAI bots](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)
- **Anthropic:** *ClaudeBot* = training. *Claude-SearchBot* = indexing for search quality (blocking "may reduce visibility"). *Claude-User* = fetches for a user's question. Hosted browsers identify as *Claude-Agent*. All honour robots.txt and Crawl-delay. IP lists at claude.com/crawling/bots.json. Anthropic will also honour an IETF *aipref* "no AI training" signal **[2026]**: [Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler)
- **Perplexity:** *PerplexityBot* = search index (not training). *Perplexity-User* = user-triggered fetch, "generally ignores robots.txt" **[2026]**: [Perplexity docs](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots)
- **Google:** *Google-Extended* is a robots.txt token (not a separate UA) controlling Gemini training and Gemini Apps / Vertex grounding. It "does not impact a site's inclusion in Google Search nor is it used as a ranking signal". AI Overviews/AI Mode are governed by Googlebot plus snippet controls (nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet, noindex). Google "intends to support the AIPREF" signal in early 2027 **[2026]**: [Google crawlers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/google-common-crawlers); [Google AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- **New Google opt-out (2026):** a Search Console control to exclude a site from grounding AI Overviews, AI Mode and AI Overviews in Discover. "Sites that opt out will not receive traffic or impressions from our generative AI features." It is not a ranking signal elsewhere. Testing began in the UK on 3 June 2026, and it had "rolled out … to all websites worldwide" by 31 Aug 2026 **[2026]**: [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-opportunities-control-and-insights-for-website-owners/)
- **Applebot-Extended** is a token controlling Apple AI training use of Applebot data, without affecting Siri/Spotlight. **CCBot** (Common Crawl) and **Bytespider** (ByteDance) are real crawlers, and Bytespider is reported to often ignore robots.txt **[SECONDARY]**: [robotstxt.com AI guide](https://robotstxt.com/ai); [Layershift](https://blog.layershift.com/which-ai-bots-to-allow)

**Cloudflare**
- 1 July 2025: new Cloudflare sign-ups block AI crawlers by default. Pay Per Crawl launched in private beta (publishers set per-crawl prices via HTTP 402). ## 4. Agent readiness: AI browsing agents, emerging standards (WebMCP, NLWeb, MCP, A2A, schema.org actions), Google agentic local features in Spain

### Takeaway
Browser agents mostly "see" a site through screenshots, the DOM and the **accessibility tree**. The one PROVEN agent-readiness action is therefore **excellent semantic, accessible HTML**: real buttons and links, labelled form fields, no overlays, stable layout. That also helps humans and WCAG compliance. **WebMCP** is the most relevant emerging standard (Chrome origin trial from Chrome 149, "subject to change"), but no mainstream consumer agent depends on it yet. NLWeb, site-hosted MCP servers, A2A and UCP are enterprise/commerce oriented and overkill for a one-lawyer site. Google's agentic local features (AI Mode booking, "let Google call", Gemini auto-browse) are **US-only or excluded from the EEA**. None is available in Spain or offered for legal services as of Nov 2026.

### Cited Findings

## 6. YMYL / legal specifics: how AI engines treat legal queries, source preferences, realistic outcome for a small local firm

### Takeaway
On legal questions AI engines favour authoritative information sources (government/official sites, big legal publishers and free legal-information portals, directories/rankings) over individual firms. In one Australian AIO analysis, government Legal Aid sites out-cited every private firm more than 10× and no firm made the top 10. Chatbots also have well-documented legal accuracy problems. For a small Spanish firm the realistic aim is not to "be the answer" to generic legal questions (BOE, administration sites and large portals will dominate). It is to (a) be correctly described and recommended for **branded and local-commercial** queries, (b) win **niche, practice-based long-tail** questions where first-hand local expertise is rare, and (c) convert the higher-intent visitors who do click through.

### Cited Findings
- 5W study (288 answers from ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity; US-centric): official/government sources made up 16.4% of citations and company/brand sites 31.9%. Leading domains: Justia 23.8%, Bloomberg Law 22.7%, Cornell LII 17.8%, Law.com 15.0% (four-engine shares) **[2026][PR-agency research]**: [5W Legal AI Visibility Report 2026](https://5wpr.com/research/Legal-AI-Visibility-Report-2026/); [Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/who-speaks-for-the-law-in-the-ai-answer)
- In 5W's "law firm" query set, directories and rankings made up 21.3% of citations and government sources 3.5% **[2026]**: [5W baseline](https://5wpr.com/research/legal/baseline/)
- In Australian Google AIO legal results, government Legal Aid sites collectively out-cite every private firm by more than 10×, and no individual law firm appeared in the top ten **[2026][agency research]**: [DNHQ](https://digitalnomadshq.com.au/?p=44276)
- SE Ranking found Google often adds professional-consultation disclaimers to YMYL (health/finance) AIOs and avoids AIOs on some sensitive topics **[2025]**: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-ai-overviews-avoid-political-content-new-data-shows/529244/). Google shows AIO only when "additive to classic Search": [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- Stanford HAI: general-purpose chatbots hallucinated 58–82% of the time on legal queries, and even dedicated legal research tools erred in 1 of 6+ queries **[OLDER: 2024]**: [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-or-more-benchmarking-queries)
- Legal-query AIO prevalence: about 23.6% (Ahrefs) vs 77.67% (SE Ranking). See section 1: [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-ai-overviews-avoid-political-content-new-data-shows/529244/)
- Spanish practitioner view: AI answers aggregate websites, profiles, reviews and directories, so a firm "not well represented in those environments simply won't appear". Prioritise content answering concrete questions, NAP consistency, reviews, legal rankings and specialised media **[2025]**: [LegalToday](https://www.legaltoday.com/gestion-del-despacho/marketing-para-abogados/como-adaptarse-a-la-nueva-busqueda-con-ia-claves-para-que-tu-despacho-sea-visible-en-todos-los-canales-2025-12-09/)
- Local AI visibility is concentrated: ChatGPT recommends only about 1.2% of local business locations **[SECONDARY]**: [Loganix](https://loganix.com/google-local-search-ranking-factors/)

### Inferences
- In Spain the equivalents of Justia/Cornell LII are likely BOE, the administration's own sites (SEPE, Seguridad Social, Agencia Tributaria, Poder Judicial/CENDOJ) and large portals (e.g., Conceptos Jurídicos, Iberley, Noticias Jurídicas, legal media). This is an inference by analogy and was not measured in this research. A DIY prompt run should verify which domains actually get cited for the firm's practice areas.
- Content strategy implication: link out to BOE and official sources (citing authoritative sources is one of the GEO methods that worked in the Princeton paper), but differentiate with what portals don't have:
  - practical local procedure ("cómo funciona en los juzgados de [ciudad]")
  - real timelines and costs ranges
  - anonymised case patterns
  - plain-language checklists
  - the lawyer's signed experience (colegiado number, years, practice)
- Expected outcome to promise the client (inference): within 6–12 months, (1) accurate, consistent answers when AI engines are asked about the firm by name; (2) appearance in AI Mode/ChatGPT/Perplexity recommendations for "abogado de [especialidad] en [ciudad]" *if* GBP, reviews and directory presence are strong; (3) occasional citations on niche informational queries; (4) measurable but small AI referral traffic (Spain-wide AI referrals are about 0.3% of traffic). Do not promise rankings in AI answers. Google itself warns against tools "that promise ranking success": [Google AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

- No data on whether Google AIO/AI Mode in Spanish adds legal disclaimers or suppresses AIO for some legal topics.

Package the service in three layers:
1. **Foundation**, production-ready, included in the website build: crawl access for AI search bots, indexability, accessible semantic HTML, GBP/Bing/entity consistency, measurement.
2. **Authority & citability**, ongoing monthly service: answer-first expert content, reviews, real mentions/PR, freshness, prompt tracking.
3. **Experimental "agent-ready" extras**, a low-cost differentiator clearly labelled as early-stage: WebMCP-annotated contact form, hand-written llms.txt, structured booking.

Anything marketed as guaranteed AI ranking, bought mentions or mass fan-out pages is hype or a policy risk.

**Things to explicitly NOT sell (hype or policy risk)**
- Guaranteed "top position in ChatGPT/AI Overviews". Google warns against tools promising ranking success.
- Bought or fake brand mentions, review gating or fake reviews. These violate Google policy and likely bar rules.
- Mass-generated pages for every query variant (scaled content abuse).
- "AI-only" content formats: markdown mirrors, chunked micro-pages, keyword-stuffed FAQ blocks.
- llms.txt as the headline deliverable.

**KPIs to report to the client (monthly)**
1. AI mention rate and own-URL citation rate across the fixed prompt set, by engine. Present as ranges given LLM variability.
2. Accuracy score of AI answers about the firm (name, address, phone, specialties).
3. GSC AI-feature impressions and the pages appearing in AI responses.
4. Bing AI Performance citations.
5. GA4 AI-assistant sessions and leads.
6. GBP calls, direction requests and bookings.
7. Self-reported "found via AI" leads.
8. Accessibility score and an agent task test (can Comet / Claude in Chrome complete "request a consultation" end-to-end?).

### Gaps
- None of the checklist items has a law-firm-specific or Spain-specific controlled study behind it. Impact ratings are judgments extrapolated from the cited general studies.
- IndexNow's effect on AI engines (Copilot/ChatGPT freshness) was not researched.
- Spanish bar advertising and ethics constraints on reviews, testimonials and "best lawyer" claims were not researched and could limit items 10–11.
- European Accessibility Act applicability to a small law firm website was not researched (it may strengthen the business case for item 6).

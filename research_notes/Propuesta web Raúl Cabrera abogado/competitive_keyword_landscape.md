# Competitive search landscape and keyword/topic opportunities: Raúl Cabrera, abogado penalista (Las Palmas de Gran Canaria)

Research date: 2026-11-09. Method notes that apply to every section below:
- **No live Google SERP or local pack could be captured.** An automated request to google.es returned HTTP 429 (captcha/"sorry" page). The web-search tool used here is US-based and is not Google Spain. Rankings described as "appears for" mean the page surfaced in this research's search tool or in directory listings, not a verified Google.es position.
- **Google autocomplete** was pulled directly from Google's public suggest endpoint with `hl=es&gl=es` (example: [suggest endpoint, "abogado penalista"](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=es&gl=es&q=abogado%20penalista)). The suggestions for generic seeds came back biased toward the Canary Islands ("abogado penalista las palmas / lanzarote / tenerife"), so the request was probably geolocated to Canarias by IP. That bias is useful for local demand, but suggestions are not national.
- **Google Trends** was pulled from the Trends explore API for the last 5 years (2021-11 to 2026-11), for both Canarias (`geo=ES-CN`) and Spain (`geo=ES`) ([Trends ES-CN example](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&geo=ES-CN&q=abogado%20penalista,alcoholemia,antecedentes%20penales); [Trends ES example](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&geo=ES&q=abogado%20penalista,alcoholemia,antecedentes%20penales,orden%20de%20alejamiento,juicio%20rapido)). Trends values are relative indexes (0–100) within each comparison set, not absolute volumes. Trends is noisy and sampled, and one request hit a 429 retry before succeeding.
- **No paid keyword tool was available.** No absolute search volumes are given anywhere in these notes. Wherever volume is discussed, it is a relative signal or an explicitly flagged inference.

## 2. Who competes in Google for "abogado penalista Las Palmas"-type queries (organic + local pack)

### Takeaway
The local criminal-law SERP is a mix of four groups. The first is a handful of **local criminal specialists** (Tommaso Garzia, Consortium Abogadis on the exact-match domain abogadopenalistalaspalmas.com, ALY Abogados, KRS Abogados). The second is **national programmatic firms** with Las Palmas landing pages (Pardo Geijo, from Murcia). The third is a thick layer of **directories and "best X in city" lists** (Melendos, elabogado.com, palmasdegrancanaria.es, Galán de Mora, Leadset Places, LegalRank, abogadosenlaspalmas.es). The fourth is **AI-generated programmatic sites** (asesor.legal) that flood long-tail local queries. Local criminal specialists are few, and most have thin content and no active blog. The best-converting site, Tommaso Garzia, has no blog at all. A focused penalista with a strong GBP and a genuinely local, frequently updated content hub can realistically compete.

## 3. National legal portals dominating informational criminal-law queries, and what that implies

### Takeaway
Informational criminal queries in Spain are dominated by a few groups: legal publishers and portals (Conceptos Jurídicos, Legálitas, Iberley, Noticias Jurídicas, Economist & Jurist, LegalToday, vLex, Mundojuridico.info); large criminal-firm blogs (e.g., Garón Abogados); price and marketplace sites (Cronoshare, Cuantomecuesta); Q&A and forums (JustAnswer, Todoexpertos, Forocoches); and, increasingly, **AI-generated programmatic sites** (asesor.legal, recordinglaw.com/es) that publish "[question] en [city]" and "(2026)" guides at scale. Of the names in the brief, Rocamora, Arriaga and Kalasa are banking or second-chance players and did not appear in criminal SERPs. A small Las Palmas penalista will not beat these portals on head terms such as "delito de lesiones" or "antecedentes penales". It can win on **local + procedural + situational** long-tail queries, and on queries where a practitioner's first-hand view (what actually happens at the Las Palmas guardia, typical conformity outcomes) beats encyclopedic content.

## 5. Hot legal topics in Spain 2025–2026 relevant to criminal defense (verified vs. not)

### Takeaway
The criminal-law reforms that are real, in force and searchable are **LO 1/2026 on multirreincidencia** (in force 10 April 2026), **LO 1/2025**'s criminal-procedure pieces (juicio rápido for usurpación and allanamiento; Tribunales de Instancia with Secciones de Violencia sobre la Mujer that absorb sexual violence), and **LO 5/2024 del Derecho de Defensa** (client information and presupuesto previo). Three topics are pending and must be framed as such: the **new LECrim** (fiscal instructor, juez de garantías), which is stalled in Congress; the **0.2 g/l alcohol limit**, which is in parliamentary processing and administrative, not criminal; and a second multirreincidencia bill. **MASC (LO 1/2025) expressly excludes criminal matters**, so it is not a criminal-defense topic. The **RD 316/2026 extraordinary regularisation**, whose window closed 30 June 2026, drove demand for "antecedentes penales". Canarias has the third-highest gender-violence complaint rate in Spain.

### Cited Findings
**LO 1/2026 (multirreincidencia):**
- Ley Orgánica 1/2026, de 8 de abril, en materia de multirreincidencia, was published in the BOE on 9 April 2026 (BOE-A-2026-7966) and is in force from 10 April 2026.

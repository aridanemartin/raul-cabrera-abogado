# Design, conversion, tech stack and pricing for a solo criminal-defence lawyer website rebuild in Spain (Raúl Cabrera, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria). Research date: 2026-11-09

## Design: what makes lawyer websites stand out in 2025–2026, which trust signals matter, and where the client is positioned now

### Takeaway
The best law-firm sites of 2025–2026 are judged on client experience, not on looks alone. They avoid the gavel-and-columns clichés, use real and distinctive branding, write in plain language, and let visitors "start working with the lawyer before they call" (FAQs, scheduling, interactive tools). Raúl's current WordPress site already has a clear niche: criminal defence across the Canary Islands, in a warm "tú" voice. It lacks hard trust signals: no colegiado number, no years of experience, no reviews, no fee guidance and no booking.

### Cited Findings
**Client's current site (quick look; the full audit is done by another researcher)**
- Positioning: criminal-defence specialist based in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, defending clients "en todo el archipiélago canario". The H1/hero reads "Experiencia. Compromiso. Resultados. Defensa Penal Profesional en Canarias." — [raulcabreraabogado.com](https://raulcabreraabogado.com/)
- Practice areas listed: Defensa Penal Personalizada, Asistencia en Investigaciones, Protección de Derechos, Delitos Menores, Delitos de Violencia (domestic violence, assault, threats) and Revisión de Sentencias. — [raulcabreraabogado.com](https://raulcabreraabogado.com/)
- Tone: warm and direct, using informal "tú" ("Contáctame", "Estoy aquí para ayudarte"). There is a "Defensa Penal en Primera Persona" section. — [raulcabreraabogado.com](https://raulcabreraabogado.com/)
- Calls to action: a clickable phone number, email, a contact form, a "Solicitar consulta" header button and a floating WhatsApp button. **No online booking.** — [raulcabreraabogado.com](https://raulcabreraabogado.com/)

Experience is only mentioned vaguely ("A lo largo de los años"). **No colegiado number, credentials, reviews or case results.** There is a NextGenerationEU / Kit Digital funding notice. **No fee information.** — [raulcabreraabogado.com](https://raulcabreraabogado.com/)

### Inferences
- Design direction for Raúl: "calm authority + human proximity".
  - Real, editorial-quality photography of Raúl (not stock gavels).
  - A restrained palette: deep navy, ink or oxblood plus a warm neutral. A single accent reserved for CTAs.
  - A serif or high-contrast display face for headings with a highly legible sans for body text.
  - Generous whitespace and plain-language copy.
  - This follows the "avoid clichés / lifestyle-brand feel / crisp typography" pattern in the Lawyerist and Converge examples. The palette and type choices are my inference; no source quantified typography trends.
- Criminal-defence visitors are often in crisis: someone was just arrested, received a citación or has a juicio rápido. The design should put **"¿Te han detenido? / ¿Tienes un juicio?" urgent-help routes** and a 24h-style contact promise above the fold. It should also add a "qué pasa ahora" process explainer (detención → asistencia letrada → declaración → juicio), FAQ blocks built from real intake questions, and plain-language guides.
- Hard trust signals to add, all of which the current site lacks:
  - Colegiado number and Colegio (ICA Las Palmas)
  - Year he began practising
  - Courts and islands covered
  - Languages he actually speaks
  - Google reviews, embedded or linked (only if compatible with deontology)
  - Media appearances and articles
  - A short video of Raúl explaining how he works
  - Fee guidance ("primera consulta X €, descontable")
- Replace the fake 8-language machine-translation widget. It signals low quality and creates thin or duplicate content risks; see the Multilingual section.

## Conversion: what converts on legal service sites, benchmarks, mobile patterns and practice-area landing pages

### Takeaway
In legal, the phone is still the main conversion channel: more than half of conversions are calls. Mobile dominates traffic. Legal landing pages convert at a median of about 6.3%. Online scheduling, short intake forms, e-signature and text messaging measurably improve conversion and time-to-hire for solo firms. Paid first consultations by video at €35–65 (deductible if hired) are an established pattern in Spain.

### Cited Findings
**Benchmarks**
- Unbounce, legal landing pages:
  - Median conversion rate 6.3% vs 6.6% across all industries. Family and disability law 6.3%, immigration 5.6%.
  - Mobile is **88% of traffic** and converts at 21% vs 15.9% on desktop.
  - Paid search median 8.3% (Google 8.6%), paid social 4.8%, email 2.7%.
  - The page does not state the data year; image filenames reference "CBR-2024". — [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/legal-conversion-rate/)
- Ruler Analytics (29 May 2026):
  - Legal average conversion rate 7.9%.
  - By channel (2026): referral 8.8%, **AI referral 8.4%**, paid search 7.8%, organic 7.3%, email 7.3%, direct 7.32%.
  - **56.3% of legal conversions happen via phone call**, the highest call share of any industry in Ruler's dataset. Its 2025 table showed lower rates (organic 3.0%, paid 4.3%), so its year-on-year methodology varies. — [Ruler Analytics](https://www.ruleranalytics.com/blog/reporting/legal-marketing-statistics/)
- Whole-site visitor-to-lead rates of roughly 2–3% are commonly cited (for example, about 2.8% for personal injury). These come from vendor blogs, so they are low confidence. — [Search summary: webtonic / sourceloop / growlaw](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/personal-injury-law-landing-page-statistics); criminal-defence vendor ranges of "6.3–15%" — [sourceloop.ai](https://sourceloop.ai/blog/criminal-defense-attorney-website-conversion-rate-benchmarks/) (vendor, unverified)

**Tools that improve conversion (Clio 2025 Legal Trends for Solo & Small Firms)**
- Firms using online schedulers saw a **15% improvement in time to hire**. Firms using digital intake tools had **20% higher revenue**.
- For solo firms, conversion improved by **10% with e-signatures, 7% with text messaging, and up to 5% with online intake forms**.
- **75% of solo firms offer flat fees**, which Clio links to clients' demand for pricing transparency.
- Source: [Clio 2025 solo/small report](https://www.clio.com/uk/guides/2025-solo-small-firm-report/). The Clio press page returned 403, so these figures come from search snippets of the Clio guide and need verifying before quoting.

**Paid first consultations in Spain (direct precedent for a "consulta por videollamada" product)**
- Garona Abogados: "videoconsulta privada desde 50 €", with a reply in 24–48 h. — [Garona](https://www.garonabogados.es/consulta-abogado-online/)
- Aguado Abogados: online consultation at **65 € IVA incluido**, paid online, with the video link sent instantly. The fee is deducted from final fees if hired. — [Aguado Abogados](https://aguadoabogados.com/consulta-legal-online/)
- Machelin Díaz: 30-minute online appointment for **35 €**, deducted if hired. — [machelindiaz.com](https://machelindiaz.com/cita-online-abogado-30min/)
- Entre Trámites: 1-hour consultation booked and paid by card on the site, refunded against later services over 250 € + IVA. — [Entre Trámites](https://entretramites.com/asesoria/consulta-especialista-civil)
- In Catalonia, video consultations are reported at 100–250 €. — [asesor.legal](https://asesor.legal/?p=176353) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Main CTA hierarchy for Raúl, on mobile first:
  1. A sticky bottom bar with **Llamar** (tel:) and **WhatsApp**, since calls are more than 50% of legal conversions and mobile is about 88% of traffic.
  2. **"Reservar videoconsulta (X €, descontable)"** with Cal.com plus Stripe.
  3. A **3–4-field callback form** (name, phone, matter type, urgency) with an RGPD checkbox.
- Avoid live chat or AI chatbots for criminal matters at launch. They need staffing or carry confidentiality and AI-Act transparency issues (handled by the compliance researcher), and the data above favours phone and scheduling. An FAQ-driven "¿qué hago si…?" guide gives most of the value without the risk.
- Each practice-area landing page should follow roughly this structure (around 600–1,000 words, plain language):
  - The problem, in the client's words
  - What Raúl does, with a step-by-step process
  - What to do right now (urgent checklist)
  - Fee approach
  - FAQs (FAQPage-style content)
  - Proof (credentials, years, courts)
  - Repeated CTAs
  - Links to related blog articles
- Track every tel:, WhatsApp and booking click as a conversion event. Otherwise more than half of conversions are invisible (see Ruler's call share).

### Gaps
- No Spain-specific conversion benchmarks for legal sites were found. All benchmark data is US/UK vendor data.
- No reliable 2025–2026 data on WhatsApp's share of legal enquiries in Spain.
- No primary data on response-time expectations of legal consumers. The Clio pages were blocked, and Ruler does not cover it.

## Tech stack: Astro vs Next.js vs WordPress (including headless) for this use case

### Takeaway
**Recommended: Astro (static-first, with server islands only where needed) + git-based content (Markdown/MDX content collections, optionally edited through Keystatic) + Cloudflare (Pages/Workers) hosting.**
- It has the best real-world Core Web Vitals pass rate of the options compared (about 67% vs about 49% for WordPress).
- It has almost no attack surface. WordPress plugin vulnerabilities hit a record in 2025, and Next.js had two critical CVEs in 2025.
- It suits an automated blog pipeline natively, since an AI pipeline can open a pull request with a Markdown file.
- Astro is now backed by Cloudflare (acquired January 2026) and remains MIT open source.

### Cited Findings
**Performance**
- HTTP Archive Core Web Vitals Technology Report, as reported by Search Engine Journal (27 May 2026). Share of origins with good CWV: Duda about 85%, Wix about 80%, Shopify about 79%, **Astro 67%**, Drupal about 64%, Joomla about 58%, **WordPress about 49%**. Next.js was not covered. — [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/core-web-vitals-wordpress-and-astro/575818/)
- Astro's own 2023 framework report had Astro at 68.8% passing INP, the closest of any framework to 80%. This is older data. — [Astro blog 2023](https://astro.build/blog/2023-web-framework-performance-report)

**Security and maintenance burden**
- Patchstack "State of WordPress Security in 2026" (2025 data, published 25 Feb 2026, via secondary coverage):
  - **11,334 new vulnerabilities** in 2025, up 42% from 7,966 in 2024.
  - About **91% were in plugins** and 9% in themes. Core vulnerabilities were negligible.
  - The weighted median time to first mass exploitation was **5 hours**. — [Patchstack whitepaper page](https://patchstack.com/whitepaper/state-of-wordpress-security-in-2026/) (figures are from secondary summaries; verify against the whitepaper)
- Next.js:
  - **CVE-2025-29927** (CVSS 9.1) is a middleware authorization bypass in self-hosted 11.1.4–15.2.2. — [UpGuard](https://www.upguard.com/blog/critical-middleware-bypass-vulnerability-in-next-js-cve-2025-29927.md)
  - **CVE-2025-55182 "React2Shell"** (CVSS 10.0) is an unauthenticated remote code execution flaw in React Server Components affecting Next.js 15.x/16.x App Router in default configurations. Patching was the only fix. — [Vercel changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/cve-2025-55182); [JFrog](https://jfrog.com/blog/2025-55182-and-2025-66478-react2shell-all-you-need-to-know)
  - A follow-up React Server Components denial-of-service issue, CVE-2026-23864, was disclosed in January 2026. — [Endor Labs](https://endorlabs.com/learn/critical-remote-code-execution-rce-vulnerabilities-in-react-and-next-js)

**Astro's status and features**
- Cloudflare announced its acquisition of The Astro Technology Company on **16 Jan 2026**. Astro stays MIT open source and deployable to any host, Cloudflare continues the Astro Ecosystem Fund (with Webflow, Netlify, Wix and Sentry), and the deal was announced alongside the Astro 6 beta. — [Cloudflare press release](https://www.cloudflare.com/press/press-releases/2026/cloudflare-acquires-astro-to-accelerate-the-future-of-high-performance-web-development/); [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/cloudflare-acquires-team-behind-open-source-framework-astro/)
- Astro content collections: a `glob()` loader reads Markdown/MDX files from a folder, and a Zod schema validates frontmatter (title, description, pubDate and so on) at build time. — [Astro docs via Context7: content collections](https://github.com/withastro/docs/blob/main/src/content/docs/en/guides/content-collections.mdx)
- The `@astrojs/cloudflare` adapter supports server islands, actions and sessions for on-demand routes. That lets you keep pages static and render only dynamic bits, such as availability or a form endpoint, on the server. — [Astro docs: Cloudflare adapter](https://github.com/withastro/docs/blob/main/src/content/docs/en/guides/integrations-guide/cloudflare.mdx)

**CMS options (2026)**
- **Keystatic**: git-based with no database and edits from GitHub or local files, described as the best git-based CMS for Astro and Next.js. Weak points: no native draft/review workflow and a limited mobile experience. — [Lucky Media comparison](https://www.luckymedia.dev/compare/keystatic-vs-decap-cms)
- **Decap CMS**: free (MIT) and mature. Previews need custom React templates, and it requires some Git knowledge, which is a barrier for non-technical editors. — [Lucky Media comparison](https://www.luckymedia.dev/compare/decap-cms-vs-keystatic)
- **Sanity**: hosted headless CMS. The free tier covers 20 seats and 10,000 documents, and the Growth plan is $15/seat/month (as of Sept 2026). It has an official `@sanity/astro` integration. The Studio can overwhelm non-technical users unless customised. — [Search summary of Lucky Media / Layer3 Labs guides](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/astro-cms)

### Inferences
- **Why not WordPress for Raúl:**
  - About half of WordPress origins fail CWV.
  - Plugin vulnerabilities are exploited within hours, so the site needs a paid maintenance plan just to stay safe.
  - An "automated blog" on WordPress means REST-API publishing into a database. That is harder to review and roll back than a git pull request.
  - Headless WordPress keeps the security and maintenance burden and adds a second system.
- **Why not Next.js:** it is overkill for a roughly 15–30-page brochure-plus-blog site. Its 2025 critical CVEs show the server-runtime attack surface. Astro ships near-zero JavaScript by default, which helps INP.
- **Editing model for a non-technical lawyer:** Raúl should not need to edit anything routinely. The monthly service handles content. Offer Keystatic (or Sanity's free tier if a richer editor UI is needed) for occasional text or photo edits. Blog articles are created by the automated pipeline as **pull requests that Raúl, or the agency, approves**. This leaves an audit trail useful for deontology and accuracy review.
- **Hosting:** Cloudflare Pages/Workers. Free or very cheap at this traffic, with a global CDN and native Astro support. Static output means hosting is portable to EU-based hosts if data residency becomes a requirement. Third-party processors (booking, analytics, forms) matter more for GDPR than static page hosting does; the compliance researcher should confirm.
- Target CWV budgets: LCP under 2.5 s, INP under 200 ms, CLS under 0.1 (Google's "good" thresholds; INP replaced FID as a Core Web Vital in March 2024). Source: [web.dev INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp). This page was not fetched in this session; the thresholds are long-standing official values.
- **Agentic SEO / AI-readiness fits a static Astro build well:**
  - Server-rendered HTML with no JS dependency for content
  - JSON-LD (LegalService/Attorney, Person, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, Article)
  - A deliberate robots.txt policy for AI crawlers
  - An optional llms.txt
  - Clean semantic headings and fast responses
  - The Ruler data shows AI referrals already converting at 8.4% in legal.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the HTTP Archive CWV dashboard directly. The Astro and WordPress pass rates are as reported by Search Engine Journal, and the data month is likely April 2026.
- Next.js CWV pass rate in the same report: not available in the source.
- A reported 2025 Web Almanac figure (48% of mobile origins pass CWV, July 2025 CrUX) came from a search summary without a verifiable URL, so I excluded it from the findings.
- I did not verify current Cloudflare Pages/Workers free-tier limits or EU-host prices (Raiola Networks hosting from 8.95 €/month excl. IVA appeared only as a search snippet).

## Integrations: booking, payments, video, analytics, tracking and practice-management software that are realistic for a solo lawyer

### Takeaway
A realistic solo-lawyer stack:
- **Cal.com** (free plan; it takes Stripe/PayPal payments) or Calendly Standard (about $10–12/seat) for paid video consultations
- **Stripe** for payments
- Google Meet or Cal Video for the call
- **Plausible** (cookieless, EU-hosted, about $9/month) or self-hosted Umami for analytics, plus Google Search Console, with events on tel:/WhatsApp/booking clicks

Practice-management software (Kleos, Sudespacho, Aranzadi Fusión) should stay separate: link out or email-forward leads rather than integrate deeply.

### Cited Findings
- **Cal.com:** Free plan at $0 (1 user) **accepts Stripe and PayPal payments** and includes email and SMS notifications. Teams costs $12/user/month and Organizations $28/user/month. Routing forms and custom workflows start at Teams. The pricing page does not mention EU data residency. — [Cal.com pricing](https://cal.com/pricing)
- **Calendly:** Standard costs about **$10/seat/month annually ($12 monthly)** and includes Stripe/PayPal payments. Teams is $16/$20. Calendly charges no fee of its own on payments, but Stripe fees apply. — [Jotform guide](https://www.jotform.com/blog/calendly-pricing/); [Cal.com blog on Calendly pricing](https://cal.com/blog/calendly-pricing) (competitor source); some outliers exist, so verify at calendly.com/pricing
- **Plausible:** Starter is **$9/month (1 site, 10k pageviews)**, with Growth at $14 and Business at $19. It uses **no cookies**, keeps data on EU-owned servers ("never leaves the EU"), and supports goals and custom events, including outbound-link and form-completion goals. The code is open source and self-hostable. — [Plausible](https://plausible.io/)
- **AEPD (Spanish DPA) guidance on audience-measurement cookies (January 2024):** these cookies can be **exempt from consent** only under strict conditions:
  - Use is limited to strictly necessary audience measurement for the publisher alone
  - No transfer or reuse by third parties
  - Disclosure in the cookie policy and a contract with the provider
  - Limited retention (25 months cited)
  - The AEPD warns that some analytics services' cookies will not qualify. — [iubenda summary](https://www.iubenda.com/es/blog/understanding-the-spanish-dpa-guide-on-audience-measurement-cookies/); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/tecnologia-medios-digitales/art/cookies-repaso-actualidad-nuevas-obligaciones); official PDF: [AEPD](https://WWW.AEPD.ES/guias/guia-cookies-analiticas-externas.pdf)
- **Spanish practice-management software:**
  - **Aranzadi Fusión** has no public price (demo or quote only) and integrates with LexNET. — [Hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/es/labs/mejor/software-gestion-despacho-abogados)
  - **Kleos (Wolters Kluwer)**: "from 39 €" per Capterra, or KleosCore "from 49 € per user" with LexNET, per a comparison site. The figures are inconsistent and unofficial. — [Capterra ES](https://www.capterra.es/software/1011070/kleos); [Hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/es/labs/mejor/software-gestion-despacho-abogados)
  - **Sudespacho.net** costs from 35.95 €/month with LexNET integration and is positioned for solo lawyers. — [Hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/es/labs/mejor/software-gestion-despacho-abogados)
- Clio-reported gains from scheduling, intake and e-signature tools are listed under Conversion. — [Clio](https://www.clio.com/uk/guides/2025-solo-small-firm-report/)

### Inferences
- **Analytics:** Plausible or Umami, set up cookieless and publisher-only, likely lets the site **drop the cookie banner entirely** if no other non-exempt cookies are used. That improves UX and LCP. GA4 needs consent under the AEPD rules, and consent rates make GA4 data partial. The compliance researcher should confirm exemption eligibility.
- **Booking:** Cal.com Free plus Stripe covers a single-lawyer paid "videoconsulta" at zero software cost, so the only cost is Stripe fees. Use Google Meet, or Cal Video to avoid a Google dependency. Self-hosting Cal.com is possible but adds maintenance; not recommended for a solo lawyer.
- **Lead routing:** form submissions go to Raúl's email plus WhatsApp notification, using a serverless endpoint (Cloudflare Worker) or a form service. No CRM is needed at this scale. If Raúl already uses Kleos, Sudespacho or Aranzadi, keep intake manual, since none of these offer simple public web-lead APIs (unverified).
- **Tracking:** tag `tel:`, `wa.me` and booking-completed events as Plausible goals, and connect Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. Track Google Business Profile calls separately in GBP Insights.

### Gaps
- Stripe's European card pricing was not fetched. The 2.9% + 30¢ figure in one source is the US rate. Verify current Stripe ES pricing (EEA cards are typically lower).
- Fathom, Umami Cloud and Matomo Cloud prices were not fetched.
- I did not verify whether Cal.com Cloud offers EU data residency. Check Cal.com's EU instance or DPA.
- I could not confirm whether Spanish practice-management tools expose lead-intake APIs or web forms.

## Multilingual: are English or German versions worth it for a criminal-defence lawyer in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria?

### Takeaway
Gran Canaria has the **lowest share of foreign-born residents in the Canary Islands (under 16%)**, and its largest foreign-born groups are Latin American Spanish speakers. A full multilingual site is not justified for resident clients. However, Gran Canaria receives very large tourist flows (about 920,000 German tourists alone in 2025), and arrested or accused tourists need a lawyer urgently. **A small, human-written English section** (urgent arrest help, the process, fees, contact) is worth it; German is a secondary option. Remove the current 8-language machine-translation menu.

### Cited Findings
- ISTAC data (Jan 2024, published Mar 2025), share of residents born outside Spain:
  - Fuerteventura almost 40%, Lanzarote over 33%, Tenerife 24.7%, **Gran Canaria under 16% (the lowest)**; the Canary Islands overall 22.6%.
  - The largest groups are from Venezuela (82,890), Cuba and Colombia. The largest European group is Italy (43,390).

## Pricing in Spain (2025–2026) and a proposed 3-tier packaging model

### Takeaway
Published Spanish ranges, mostly from vendor and platform sources:
- **Custom corporate/professional website:** about €1,200–6,000 (freelance about €500–2,000; agency €2,500+)
- **Local SEO retainers:** €400–1,200/month for small or local projects
- **Maintenance:** €50–150/month
- **GEO / AI-visibility services:** audits about €1,000–3,000, small-business retainers about €299–1,000/month, or a **20–30% surcharge** on an existing SEO retainer

There is no rigorous Spanish survey. All figures are indicative and usually quoted excluding tax.

### Cited Findings

- GoDaddy ES: a corporate WordPress site costs about **€1,500–2,000**, a professional one-page site €800–1,400 and a simple page €300–600. A domain costs €10–30 a year. — [GoDaddy ES](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/es/crearweb/cuanto-cuesta-crear-una-pagina-web)

- Offer a minimum term (for example 6 months) for the SEO tiers. Spanish GEO and SEO vendors use 3-month minimums, and SEO results take months.
- Frame ROI with Raúl's own economics. One criminal-defence case typically far exceeds a month's retainer, and paid consultations at €35–65 offset part of the cost.
- **Tax note (inference, not sourced):** Raúl is in the Canary Islands, which uses **IGIC (7% general rate)** instead of IVA. Quotes should say "impuestos indirectos no incluidos" and the correct tax treatment should be confirmed with an advisor, depending on where the provider is established.
- Automated content for a lawyer must include human legal review. It is an accuracy and E-E-A-T issue, carries deontology and advertising risk, and the market itself flags cheap unreviewed "SEO para IA" as a red flag (geotoolbox).

### Gaps
- **No formal 2025–2026 Spanish survey of freelance or agency web-design prices** was found. All figures come from platforms and vendors with commercial interests.
- **No Spanish agency publishes law-firm-specific website prices** in the sources found, apart from subscription products (Zunapro) and US-dollar lists.
- **No reliable Spain-specific per-article or per-word blog pricing** was found. The per-word figures are international and in USD.
- GEO prices come from vendors who sell GEO and disclose the conflict. The two sources disagree on freelance hourly rates (€40–80 vs €90–150).
- Ahrefs and SE Ranking SEO surveys are global and from 2023–2024 (older data).
- The tax treatment (IGIC vs IVA) for a mainland provider invoicing a Canary Islands client was not researched.
